# Biliary duct fidelity scope and clinical boundaries

The biliary duct pathology investigation now has a draft inventory of 306 anatomical/pathological targets and 918 image/schematic/model obligations. All remain missing or unverified; a complete walkthrough, parent duct mesh or image count is not clinical coverage.

Scope includes segment I–VIII duct trees and subsegmental branches; right/left and anterior/posterior sectoral ducts; hilar, common hepatic, supraduodenal/retroduodenal/intrapancreatic/intramural common bile duct and cystic duct segments; variant insertions and communications; pancreaticobiliary outlet and host anatomy; each actual stricture, cystic compartment and filling defect; intraductal/periductal/mass-forming tumour regions; arterial/portal branches and affected interfaces; lobar change and acquired disease sites; postoperative anastomoses, drains/stents and excluded segments. Every actual case branch, device, variant and observation still requires explicit instantiation, native sampling and independent anatomical review.

The effective reader checklist, templates, walkthrough and reading sources are fingerprinted. Normal source anatomy cannot provide stone, tumour, thrombus, biloma or metastatic-deposit examples. CT cannot supply MRI diffusion/MRCP signal or ultrasound acoustic shadows. Conditional radiographic cholangiography retains its own actual contrast/procedural source requirement; an ordinary radiograph or static projection cannot establish dynamic filling, patency or all duct boundaries. The shared renderer is now explicitly schematic context, with no patient-specific branching, walls, strictures or stone credit.

Clinical boundaries were checked against [Tokyo Guidelines 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/jhbp.512), the [BSG cholangiocarcinoma guideline](https://doi.org/10.1136/gutjnl-2023-330029) and the investigation's [original reading source](https://radiologyassistant.nl/abdomen/biliary-system/biliary-duct-pathology). The source's older surgical and classification statements still need full framework reconciliation; they are not automatic current operative verdicts.

The walkthrough no longer prefills absent surgical history/stents or excludes cholangitis from imaging alone. It now separates visible postoperative/device anatomy, abscess findings and clinical/laboratory infection assessment. Hilar ductal classification describes anatomical distribution rather than complete staging or standalone resectability. Stricture length and morphology remain observations with overlapping causes, not unique benign/malignant diagnoses. The shared lesson no longer describes length as its single most useful discriminator or supplies a universal diagnostic cutoff.

The changing-stricture quiz explanation no longer calls every concerning change proved malignant or states that inflammatory overlap is impossible. The urgent infected-obstruction quiz retains its urgent clinical response while removing automatic TG18 Grade III assignment from a low blood-pressure reading alone; defined cardiovascular support or other organ dysfunction requires separate clinical evidence. This does not delay urgent care to obtain a morphology-based severity label.

Remaining work includes original high-fidelity duct/branch/wall/vascular anatomy, actual positive lesion and complication sources, rights and native protocol validation, every relevant variant and site, specialist anatomical/clinical review and the rest of the shared gallbladder/stone lesson. No original image or model was acquired/promoted by this scope turn. The full all-radiology denominator remains unknown.
