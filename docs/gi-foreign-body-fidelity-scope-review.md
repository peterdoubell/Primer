# GI foreign-body fidelity scope review — 2026-10-04

The multimodality GI foreign-body investigation now defines 355 draft targets and 1,065 image, schematic and model obligations. The scope preserves each actual object's complete form, ends, sharp tips, edges, components and calibrated dimensions separately from its source-covered GI segment, wall/lumen relationships, injury, collection, adjacent organs/vessels and chronological source comparisons. Conditional upper-GI, thoracic, postoperative and distant host sites require actual coverage and relevance.

Radiographic visibility, projection, overlapping objects and magnification/calibration limits have their own requirements. CT object-axis planes, attenuation/window visibility, wall/organ/vessel depth and opacification have distinct requirements. A radiograph cannot supply a CT traversed-wall relationship; a CT cannot automatically supply a radiographic projection. A normal parent bowel model cannot represent the object, its true endpoints or its injury interfaces. Every actual object and case-specific site still requires explicit instantiation and independent review.

Reader presets retain adequate, limited and unassessed alternatives for object detection, bowel effect, local injury/collections and adjacent-organ/migration assessment. They no longer create blanket exclusion of ingestion, perforation or vessel/organ involvement. Object dimensions are measured only from suitable source planes and calibration, with unseen endpoints, projection/magnification, partial volume and artefacts retained as limits. Material and total object number remain source-context interpretations rather than consequences of opacity or model colour alone.

Primary sources: [Kuzmich et al., poorly conspicuous foreign-body perforation](https://doi.org/10.1259/bjr.20150086), the official [ESGE 2016 adult upper-GI guideline](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0042-100456), and the existing [Radiology Assistant sharp-object source](https://radiologyassistant.nl/abdomen/bowel/sharp-foreign-bodies-in-gi-tract). The source review confirms that subtle objects can be missed and contained/early injuries can occur without substantial free gas. Projected proximity is not independently confirmed penetration. Changed projection, field or bowel position is not proven migration without reliable source correspondence.

Prompt communication for perforating sharp objects and threatened vessels is retained, with high-risk suspected oesophageal/battery/magnet/obstructive findings qualified by the applicable age/location-specific clinical framework. The cited adult 2016 guidance is not silently applied to paediatric or lower-GI treatment. No universal endoscopy timing, retrieval rule, follow-up schedule, scan-phase prescription or operative verdict is introduced. The wider shared tumour/foreign-body lesson, quiz, management and population guidance still require complete independent reconciliation.
