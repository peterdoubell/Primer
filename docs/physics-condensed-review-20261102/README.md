# Type-I transition and ideal-cylinder field review

The existing condensed-matter model samples a discontinuous order-index transition at uniformly spaced temperatures and joins them with a sloped segment. The prototype separates superconducting and normal branches at the analytical transition temperature, uses explicit kelvin/millitesla axes, and does not assign a unique homogeneous order or field at coexistence.

The order index and critical-field law are illustrative phenomenological choices, not microscopic predictions for lead. The field geometry is an ideal infinite cylinder in longitudinal field, with zero penetration depth and negligible demagnetization. Rings bound the display window only. The prototype does not model finite end fields, type-II vortices, hysteresis, depairing or the intermediate-state domain structure.

The energy sign is checked against branch stability. A factored expression avoids cancellation near the model boundary. Original cancellation diagnostics and the corrected proof are retained separately. Magnetic normal-flux continuity and the tangential jump/surface-current relation are checked, including an independent Python calculation. Rendered field glyphs have declared value scales distinct from the millimetre coordinate scale.

This packet is a standalone research preview. It is not integrated into the application or deployed. No completion of the broader physics or radiology objective follows from these checks.

Source context: NIST, Standards for Measurement of the Critical Fields of Superconductors, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6687606/ ; CERN superconductivity overview, https://home.cern/science/physics/superconductivity/ .
