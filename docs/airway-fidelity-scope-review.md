It also captures lobular localisation, bronchiectasis, stenosis, plugs/nodules, wall patterns, mosaic attenuation, dynamic collapse, obstruction, downstream disease, extrinsic causes, developmental/situs variants, post-treatment anatomy and vascular contexts. Every actual subsegment, finding, intervention and acquired adjacent structure still requires explicit instantiation.

The linked [segment anatomy](https://radiologyassistant.nl/chest/lung-anatomy/lung-segments) and [large-airway source](https://radiologyassistant.nl/chest/airways/large-airway-disease) were consulted for draft scope. The common source scheme has ten right and eight left entries, with combined left apicoposterior anatomy and no mandatory separate LB7. Naming and branching variants remain conditional; this does not force that scheme onto every patient. A lingular region is retained within the left upper lobe, not invented as a third left lobe. Segmental territory boundaries, venous/lymphatic localisation, subsegmental anatomy and embedded source labels remain review work. This scope review grants no source-image reuse rights.

CT-visible small-airway localisation must not be confused with directly resolved microscopic bronchioles or histological walls. Static schematic regions cannot establish expiratory air trapping, dynamic collapse, calibrated bronchoarterial ratios, perfusion or vascular patency. Each has a separate functional/acquisition evidence obligation. Source/case extent, pathology, intervention and developmental conditions remain explicit blockers to completion.

It now leaves acquisition adequacy/not-acquired and assessment choices as prompts. The explicit not-acquired finding phrase remains available. This preserves the clinical question rather than filling an unsupported negative result.

No generic lung parent or static guide is awarded segment coverage. Appropriate source anatomy, complete case images and geometry still need acquisition and clinical validation.
