# Music review and Grade 1–8 path

## Findings

The previous music spine had listening/movement introductions, one notation
lesson and one broad theory lesson before undergraduate composition. It did
not provide an eight-grade progression, higher-grade harmony or a systematic
connection between written work, listening, sight-reading and practical study.
The notation plate used arcs and a straight line to approximate a treble clef.
Music foundations also depended on unrelated visual-art lessons.

## Changes

- Retain the existing lesson IDs and earned credit; the reading introduction is
  not relabelled as an earned Grade 1 qualification.
- Add eight sequential grade modules: Grades 1–3 at the intermediate school
  stage and Grades 4–8 at the secondary stage. ABRSM grades are skill levels,
  not school years or age restrictions. All eight precede undergraduate
  composition, which now explicitly depends on Grade 8 and music history.
- Each grade has three focused study units, aural work, sight-reading,
  instrument/voice practice, composition, a balanced practice routine and a
  completion checkpoint. The existing article reading remains available.
- Add 80 original assessed questions (eight choices and two produced responses
  per grade) and eight original listening comparisons. Playback is optional,
  local, stoppable and accompanied by a written explanation.
- Use previous-stage music nodes for music gates; explicit prerequisites still
  apply. Completing drawing or painting cannot substitute for a preceding
  music grade. The general curriculum's other gates retain their policy.
- Replace the notation overview with professionally engraved treble and bass
  clefs, reference notes and middle-C ledger lines. Add eight grade-specific
  notation plates with the same paper, ink and accent colours as the Primer.
  Bravura is bundled for reproducible offline rendering under the SIL OFL.

## Curriculum comparison

“Royal Schools” is interpreted as ABRSM. This is a general musicianship and
written-theory path. A specific instrument was not selected, so repertoire,
technical patterns and practical assessment must follow that instrument's
current syllabus with appropriate tuition. The listening comparisons are
worked examples, not a complete official aural-test bank. Early composition
is enrichment; formal harmony and composition become central at Grades 6–8.
A Primer quiz result establishes written knowledge, not an instrumental
performance pass or an ABRSM certificate.

| Grade | Written study benchmark | Practical connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Treble/bass; basic durations/rests, dots/ties; simple metre; C/G/D/F major; tonic triads; expression | Steady pulse, pitch matching, short phrases, introductory sight-reading |
| 2 | Ledger lines; triplets and wider simple metres; further major keys and harmonic minor; relative keys | Rhythm with rests, minor/major comparison, technical and phrase control |
| 3 | Compound metre; octave transposition; keys through four accidentals; both minor forms; interval quality | Stable compound pulse, phrase echoing, fluent unfamiliar reading |
| 4 | Alto clef; chromatic spelling; duplets/double dots; keys through five accidentals; I/IV/V; ornaments and instruments | Contrasting articulation, musical detail, phrasing and chord hearing |
| 5 | Tenor clef; transposing instruments; irregular metre; keys through six accidentals; compound intervals; inversions and cadences | Complete score reading, performance reflection and aural fluency |
| 6 | Diatonic triads/inversions; V7 and ii7; figured realisation; harmonisation, modulation and instrumental continuation | Voice-leading in sound, related-key direction, teacher-reviewed writing |
| 7 | Secondary sevenths; diminished seventh/Neapolitan sixth; figured inner parts; suspensions and decorated continuation | Prepared dissonance, bass-led hearing, independent phrasing and revision |
| 8 | Diatonic/chromatic harmony; two-treble continuo writing; keyboard and instrumental completion; score analysis | Independent parts, stylistic decisions, sustained performance and portfolio feedback |

The Grade 6–8 theory specification was read as the ABRSM-authored PDF mirrored
by Mozart Academy; the current ABRSM overview confirms the higher-grade focus.
The syllabus links below are retained for checking scope. No exam questions or
copyrighted repertoire have been reproduced.
