# Accessory soleus source figures — 30 September 2026

The current reader has superseded the repository previews described below with complete figures rendered from the original published PDF. See [the PDF source review](msk-accessory-pdf-source-review.md) for larger native PDF panels, preserved annotations, exact hashes and rendering details. The partial anatomical scope and unresolved Figure 3f plane are unchanged.

Two complete, unedited repository JPEGs from Plečko et al., [Case Reports in Orthopedics 2020:8851920](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7516695/), now support partial MRI candidates in the ankle reader. The original article gives an unrestricted Creative Commons Attribution grant. Its deposited notice and repository metadata omit the version, but the publisher-deposited [Crossref record for this exact DOI](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1155/2020/8851920) explicitly identifies CC BY 4.0. That original metadata is preserved with its hash; the version is not inferred from publication date or other papers. No separate excluded figure credit was found in the inspected captions or pixels. Exact article/XML/archive/image hashes and source captions are in [the acquisition record](msk-accessory-soleus-source-review/acquisition-and-context.json).

Figure 1 is a symptomatic right ankle in a 25-year-old woman. MRI c–f shows the labelled accessory belly, local course and a muscular medial-calcaneal insertion. Photograph a and radiograph b remain separately typed. Figure 3 is a symptomatic right ankle in a 31-year-old woman whose case also documents tarsal tunnel syndrome. MRI b–f shows the accessory muscle and a tendinous insertion; radiograph a is separate context. Neither case is a normal-examination reference or a universal insertion map.

Figure 3f is captioned as axial T1 MRI, but the displayed anatomy appears longitudinal. The original caption is preserved, and the exact plane is explicitly unresolved. This discrepancy must be reconciled before a plane-specific clinical approval. Image availability and a compatible variant name do not prove full anatomical fidelity.

The original 639×423 and 643×423 JPEGs retain all panel letters, arrows and asterisks. The licensed Europe PMC image ZIP contains no PDF or higher-resolution alternatives. The publisher DOI and the independently documented Europe PMC PDF route returned HTTP 403; those routes were stopped. A higher-resolution publisher master remains unverified. No scaling, crop, recompression, colour conversion or annotation change was made.

Each source provides only partial candidates for the accessory soleus belly, visible course and distal attachment if covered. Proximal origin, complete distal footprints, measured tissue dimensions and 3D geometry are not established. Full clinical/anatomical approval remains pending. The full MSK requirement scope is retained.
