## Verdict: 6.0/10, not 8

### Three most serious reasons not higher

1. **Some production questions confidently teach and grade the wrong answer; other orderings have no uniquely determined answer.** Generic authored teaching notes do not repair this. `data/practice/young.json:6213` asks oldest first, but sequences at 6223 and 6235 run baby → child → grown-up (→ old person). With `practice.R.seed(19)`, the second `_know_order(young_material()['hist.0.family'])` produces the three-member item; `quiz.score_quiz([q], ['a grown-up a child a baby'])` returns **0.0**, while the reversed, incorrect key returns **1.0**. Two of the node's five sequences have this reversed grading. `arts.1.instruments`, lines 8638–8661, asks lowest sound first but includes piccolo → flute → clarinet → bassoon, and triangle → snare drum → bass drum. `chem.1.matter`, lines 4994–5005, asks most tightly packed first but keys ice before liquid water. The explanation simply applies a solid/liquid/gas generalisation to the notable counterexample.

   Ambiguity remains at `young.json:8372–8390`: stand still → sway → spin → leap and tiptoe → step → stamp under “Put the dance steps in order”, without a specified choreography. The new explanation says “Follow this named movement pattern”, but none is named. `young.json:5942–5961` asks for a program's block order without specifying the program's intended output: turn right, move forward, and say hello can be composed in several legitimate orders. `young.json:1715–1741` orders blowing/pushing/kicking and press/push/shove by force without specifying force. A gentle kick can be weaker than a strong push. Spoken metadata also ignores per-sequence prompt overrides: `practice.py:1400–1401` always uses the node-wide `sequence_say`. For example the family's “earliest first” timeline still speaks oldest person to youngest. This directly affects the pre-reader audience.

2. **Review feedback is not recorded as answer exposure.** `/api/review` (`primer/server.py:2634–2640`) only calls `learner.review_card`; `primer/learner.py:1958` never refreshes the quiz fingerprint burn. Yet `web/app.js:4204–4223` displays/speaks the answer on review, including failed reviews. Reproduction with a real isolated store: save a five-year-old profile, generate a durable plants ordering, add its card and burn its fingerprint on day 0, advance wall time three days, fail its due review with quality 1. Afterwards `burned_map('bio.0.plants', window_days=2)` is **{}** and `_drop_burned([q], [q['answer']], 'bio.0.plants', 1)` keeps **1/1** immediately. The learner can use an answer just revealed after a failed review as supposedly cold mastery evidence. This is an ordinary full-loop path, not a client-forged key.

3. **Outcome validation is narrow and mostly assumes the teaching outcome.** The 45-day simulation uses one node (`bio.0.plants`), four seeds per mode, and changes a prompt to permanently known after two exposures regardless of the explanation text (`tests/test_loop_ladder_contract.py:118–133`). The adaptation experiment likewise calculates delayed errors as `exposures[p] < 2` at line 222; it does not perform an actual held-out delayed probe. This is useful evidence that scheduling changes exposure under a stated learning model, not independent evidence that the authored explanations teach, that knowledge transfers, or that the whole application improves retention. It cannot detect the wrong family answer above, since it treats the application's answer as truth. Coverage should include other nodes and plausible retention/forgetting assumptions; full browser loop reproduction remains the implementer's report, not independently observed here.
