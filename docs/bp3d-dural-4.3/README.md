# Dural venous sinuses and deep veins

The study is linked from Venous and CSF Imaging, Intracranial Haemorrhage and both reporting references. It uses 20 original objects from the version-stamped [BodyParts3D 4.3](https://lifesciencedb.jp/bp3d/) archive under [CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.1/jp/).

The primary archive CRCs and source object headers were verified for the first 12 objects. The browser encoding retains all 46,824 source faces with their original vertex order and Float32 positions in the source coordinate frame. The geometry inventory records boundary edges by index and by exact vertex coordinates. Index boundaries in five objects coincide at duplicate vertex positions. Every edge has two incident faces after grouping identical positions for analysis. Runtime indices retain the original seams. Coordinate closure does not establish continuity between different vessels.

The archive supplies right-sided transverse, sigmoid, cavernous and petrosal objects. The same FJ4654 geometry appears under the source right great cerebral vein and its parent concept. Anatomical continuity, unrepresented lateral counterparts, clinical variants and patient thrombus require separate evidence. Independent specialist review remains open.

The projection plate uses all original faces at the browser’s Float32 precision in three orthographic atlas planes. Each view includes a scale bar. The proof records a digest of the projected coordinates for each object. Depth is collapsed and overlap alone provides no evidence of a junction.

The venous sinus thrombosis walkthrough opens the available sinus or deep-vein group in a separate tab. Both presets were verified by their selected object IDs and rendered triangle totals. The delivery check verified all 18 assets. The reader rejected a valid gzip containing modified geometry and restored the selected group after a successful retry.

The accompanying angiography page preserves four complete published figures under CC BY 4.0. Oliveira and colleagues’ Figure 1 identifies seven dural-sinus and deep-vein structures. Sadik and colleagues’ Figures 5 and 6 identify basal, septal, thalamostriate and internal cerebral veins. Figure 5 contains an axial panel although its original caption also mentions a sagittal plane. The reader discloses that mismatch. The original images are separate from the BodyParts3D geometry and provide no patient-to-atlas registration. The source receipt records image and decoded-pixel hashes, author labels, article attribution and licence evidence.

The eight additional objects represent the right basal, superior anastomotic, inferior anastomotic and superficial middle cerebral veins. The primary service timed out during this acquisition. Their unaltered OBJ bytes were acquired from a pinned community mirror, with Git LFS hashes and sizes matched to the checksum-verified Zenodo archive. Requested memberships agree with the saved original 4.3 manifest after line-ending normalization. Twelve overlapping mirror objects match the earlier primary downloads byte for byte. The new objects lack a fresh primary-endpoint comparison. The receipt records this distinction.

Sadik Figure 3 is digital subtraction angiography and identifies the superficial middle cerebral, Trolard and Labbé veins with coloured arrows. Its patient side is unspecified. The right-sided atlas objects and this image retain their independent identities.
