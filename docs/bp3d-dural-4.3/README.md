# Dural venous sinuses and deep veins

The study is linked from Venous and CSF Imaging, Intracranial Haemorrhage and both reporting references. It uses 21 original objects from the version-stamped [BodyParts3D 4.3](https://lifesciencedb.jp/bp3d/) archive under [CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.1/jp/).

The primary archive CRCs and source object headers were verified for the first 12 objects. The browser encoding retains all 46,998 source faces with their original vertex order and Float32 positions in the source coordinate frame. The geometry inventory records boundary edges by index and by exact vertex coordinates. In the original twelve objects, index boundaries in five objects coincide at duplicate vertex positions. Every edge has two incident faces after grouping identical positions for analysis. Runtime indices retain the original seams. Coordinate closure does not establish continuity between different vessels.

The archive supplies right-sided transverse, sigmoid, cavernous and petrosal objects. The same FJ4654 geometry appears under the source right great cerebral vein and its parent concept. Anatomical continuity, unrepresented lateral counterparts, clinical variants and patient thrombus require separate evidence. Independent specialist review remains open.

The projection plate uses all original faces of the first twelve source objects at the browser’s Float32 precision in three orthographic atlas planes. Each view includes a scale bar. The proof records a digest of the projected coordinates for each object. Depth is collapsed and overlap alone provides no evidence of a junction.

The venous sinus thrombosis walkthrough opens the available sinus, deep-vein or cortical-vein group in a separate tab. All presets were verified by their selected object IDs and rendered triangle totals. The delivery check verifies all 41 assets. The reader rejected a valid gzip containing modified geometry and restored the selected group after a successful retry.

The accompanying angiography page preserves eleven complete published figures under CC BY 4.0. Oliveira and colleagues’ Figure 1 identifies seven dural-sinus and deep-vein structures. Sadik and colleagues’ Figures 5 and 6 identify basal, septal, thalamostriate and internal cerebral veins. Figure 5 contains an axial panel although its original caption also mentions a sagittal plane. The reader discloses that mismatch. The original images are separate from the BodyParts3D geometry and provide no patient-to-atlas registration. The source receipt records image and decoded-pixel hashes, author labels, article attribution and licence evidence.

The eight additional objects represent the right basal, superior anastomotic, inferior anastomotic and superficial middle cerebral veins. The primary service timed out during this acquisition. Their unaltered OBJ bytes were acquired from a pinned community mirror, with Git LFS hashes and sizes matched to the checksum-verified Zenodo archive. Requested memberships agree with the saved original 4.3 manifest after line-ending normalization. Twelve overlapping mirror objects match the earlier primary downloads byte for byte. The new objects lack a fresh primary-endpoint comparison. The receipt records this distinction.

Sadik Figure 3 is digital subtraction angiography and identifies the superficial middle cerebral, Trolard and Labbé veins with coloured arrows. Its patient side is unspecified. The right-sided atlas objects and this image retain their independent identities.

Three additional complete clinical figures show author-described venous thrombosis. Sadik Figures 12 and 17 compare selected MRV and CT panels, respectively. Oliveira Figure 7 pairs MRV with susceptibility-weighted MRI in an explicitly described patient with deep venous thrombosis and left caudate/thalamic haemorrhagic infarction. The straight sinus is explicitly absent from that figure. These selected images supply no native volume, registration or full thrombus measurement.

The independent audit of the additional eight meshes confirmed their source records, coordinate-edge topology and absence of duplicate faces. FJ4695 has eight closed components and FJ5007 has two. The relationships between these components require anatomical review.

The right jugular bulb model adds 174 original faces from FJ5008, the BodyParts3D 4.3 part_of representation BP29775. The source receipt verifies archive and object identity. Two complete CC BY 4.0 coronal CBCT figures by Tudose and colleagues illustrate jugular bulb landmarks and variants. The CBCT images and 3D model have independent provenance. Left-sided 3D anatomy and clinical review remain open.

Zuurbier Figure 2 compares temporal horn enlargement on FLAIR MRI and nonenhanced CT in two separate patients. The Mass effect step opens the complete CC BY 4.0 figure with separate panel descriptions.

Liu Figure 1 compares a slender left transverse sinus across three MRI panels with a separate patient with author-reported thrombosis. The Mimics and comparison step opens the complete publisher image.
