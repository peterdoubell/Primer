# Endometrial MRI scope reconciliation — 11 October 2026

The investigation previously had five reporting steps and two remote figure roles. The tumour-length figure was used for invasion depth, and neither remote role had a recorded commercial redistribution grant. Both are held in `endometrial-held-legacy-images.json`; the effective investigation explicitly has no inherited key-image fallback. The shared uterine lesson retains its separately reviewed original previews.

Nine assessment areas now cover tumour/source adequacy, same-site myometrial depth, cervical canal/stroma, subserosa/serosa/adnexa, vagina and adjacent organs, nodal stations, peritoneal/distant coverage, planning/variants/mimics, and an actual supplied treatment/comparison examination. Assessment prompts require acquired findings, methods and uncertainty instead of prefilling normal conclusions. The comparison area is conditional; baseline recurrence cases cannot establish serial response.

The [updated ESUR guideline](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-025-11700-3), published online in 2025 and assigned to the 2026 volume, supports multiphase contrast-enhanced MRI when assessing any myometrial invasion. The worksheet records missing phases and contrast contraindications. Gadolinium-free T2/DWI assessment retains its lower confidence for excluding superficial invasion. Source-defined phase identities remain separate; a reference image is not a patient's dynamic acquisition.

The [primary FIGO committee publication](https://doi.org/10.1002/ijgo.14923) distinguishes pelvic peritoneal disease (IIIB2), abdominal peritoneal disease beyond the pelvis (IVB), and other distant metastases (IVC), including nodes above the renal vessels. The versioned staging reference now gives IVA, IVB and IVC separate rows and explicit renal-vessel level context. Imaging anatomy alone cannot supply histology, grade, LVSI, pathological nodal burden or molecular inputs. The [corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1002/ijgo.15193) corrects the 0.2 mm nodal boundary; MRI node measurements are not pathological deposit sizes.

The [2026 ESR practice recommendations](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-026-12705-2) describe useful acquisition and anatomical assessment but contain a nodal-paragraph discrepancy: above-renal-vessel nodes are labelled IVB. The worksheet uses the primary FIGO distinction and records the discrepancy. The assigned [Radiology Assistant reading](https://radiologyassistant.nl/abdomen/unsorted/mr-in-endometrial-cancer) includes atypical enhancement, uterine size/position, benign mimics and fertility planning. Its universal surgical-route inference from a uterine size threshold is not adopted. Planning decisions require the actual clinical context.

Independent review also identified prose discrepancies in the [2025/2026 MRI review](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00261-025-05058-9): its change list describes IIB as myometrial invasion independent of aggressiveness, and its IA3 passage requires no LVSI. The primary committee distinguishes IIB (substantial LVSI with nonaggressive histology) from IIC (aggressive histology with any myometrial involvement); IA3 excludes substantial/extensive LVSI. These secondary descriptions are not imported as current criteria. Original figure captions and historical source interpretations remain intact and unendorsed.

Primary FIGO also distinguishes molecular modification of early-stage categories from recording molecular notation in stages III/IV without changing their category. The original Figure11 molecular caption in PMC11119523 is preserved; authored limits clarify this distinction instead of treating all advanced-stage molecular notation as prohibited.

The exact original HRA female-pelvic source entry is associated with this investigation and deduplicated in the shared parent lesson. Its native surfaces and recorded source conflicts remain unchanged. This association supplies gross reference anatomy; it does not validate junctional-zone, endometrial, myometrial or bowel-wall layers, lesions, patient axes, measurements or MRI registration.

The named fine-scope inventory is a record of missing obligations, not clinical certification. Every actual side, tissue boundary, lesion and reported interface still needs separately evidenced clinical imagery, schematics and 3D. Original published figures require source-preservation review before binding; all fine-layer and clinical approvals remain pending.

Original PDF page placement can slightly stretch a figure relative to its native pixel aspect (independent review observed about 0.234% in the Cureus examples). Runtime retains original image samples and their natural aspect, rather than reproducing the full page layout. Published display annotations do not provide independently verified measurement calibration.
