# Original dynamic swallowing MRI movie

The supplementary movie from [Voskuilen and colleagues, *Dynamic MRI of swallowing*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9188511/) supports an attributed MRI teaching example of a swallowing movement. It does not establish complete anatomical coverage, quantitative patient function, aspiration absence or a VFSS reference standard. It cannot supply a native 3D surface or a patient-space volume without the original MR arrays and spatial metadata.

The 1,322,019-byte original MP4 matches its publisher MD5 `fa90605121a6318faa2c31892c1756f1` and SHA-256 `c02a17b4ff007b6dafb73463e762e4f1e8cbd684ef8ce985a38b03c1f070fa27`. The primary XML, supplementary media manifest and original movie were reviewed. The article licence is [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/); no separate third-party restriction is attached to this supplementary movie. Attribution should identify Luuk Voskuilen, Jasper Schoormans, Oliver J. Gurney-Champion, Alfons J. M. Balm, Gustav J. Strijkers, Ludi E. Smeele and Aart J. Nederveen, the DOI and licence. An unchanged source download can be offered; decoded RGB posters and review thumbnails must be identified as display derivatives.

## Encoded display and timing

The sole stream is MPEG-4 Part 2, `mp4v`, YUV420p, 1,233 × 1,084 pixels, with no audio. All 64 frames decoded successfully. Every decoded RGB frame is distinct by its full-frame digest; this does not prove independent acquisition. Presentation timestamps are exactly `i × 1000 / 12346` seconds for zero-based frame index `i=0…63`. The interval is about 0.080998 seconds, the last frame starts at 5.102867 seconds and the encoded duration is 5.183865 seconds. The encoded rate `6173/500` is 12.346 frames per second. It is a presentation rate, not proof of native VFSS timing or independent MRI temporal resolution. No frame interpolation, retiming, enhancement or anatomical crop was introduced in the decode review.

The image contains seven sagittal-style views in a **2 / 3 / 2 panel arrangement**: two across the upper row, three across the middle row and two across the lower row. The source caption defines sequential slice order as left to right, then top to bottom. All seven views remain in every decoded frame. There are no printed panel numbers, patient identifiers, side markers, physical scale bars or in-picture clocks. The source does not identify the individual volunteer represented in this movie. The arrangement is an already rendered montage; the panel ordering does not recover raw image arrays, a patient affine or independent physical registration.

## Visual observations

All 64 frames were inspected in four full-montage contact sheets. Native-size frames 1, 19, 23, 26–37, 40, 44, 48, 52 and 64 were additionally inspected around the principal visible change and the endpoints. The contact sheets preserve the complete source frame as nearest-neighbour thumbnails; their labels are review labels outside the image, not new source acquisition timestamps.

Early frames show a conspicuous bright fluid region above the tongue in several panels. Its outline changes and becomes less conspicuous in the middle oral views around frames 29–37, while the tongue/palate relationship changes. Later frames retain the same seven views with a much less conspicuous oral bright-fluid pool. The image is soft and visibly coarse within each panel. Some boundaries and neck signal are difficult to separate, and encoded intensity changes are apparent at the beginning. Neither global pixel differences nor a disappearing bright region determines a physiological event boundary.

These observations support showing the movie as an example of dynamic MRI. They do **not** independently prove bolus propulsion efficiency, epiglottic sealing, complete airway protection, upper oesophageal sphincter opening, clearance, absence of residue or aspiration. Exact oral/pharyngeal/oesophageal/rest onset frames are unverified. The article's phase interpretation should remain attributed to its authors; no new phase labels, patient measurements or normal templates should be inferred from this review.

## Source acquisition limits

The article describes five healthy adult volunteers, one female, ages 26–29 years (mean 28). That cohort information does not assign the movie an individual age, sex or subject number. Volunteers swallowed 20 mL pineapple juice supine. The reported 3 T MR acquisition used seven slices at 2 × 2 × 6 mm (AP × FH × RL); the 21-slice experiment was a phantom experiment and does not describe this volunteer movie. The reconstruction used spatial and temporal regularisation, a flat-field filter and two half-frame-shifted sliding-window reconstructions combined to double the nominal 6.2 fps rate. Neighbouring presented frames therefore must not be described as independent 12 fps samples. The original reconstruction and compression are retained in this file; they cannot be undone by enlarging a panel.

The supplement caption says the movie is represented in “figure 6”. The primary article XML contains Figures 1–4, and the results paragraph links Figure 4 and Online Resource 2. This is a confirmed source-numbering discrepancy. It remains explicit in the proof and must not be silently presented as a verified Figure 6 correspondence. Figure 4 supplies the authors' interpretation of four phases; its three illustrated slices are not all seven movie panels.

## Runtime decision

An unchanged original movie, or an explicitly identified browser-compatible display transcode checked against all decoded frames and presentation timing, can be integrated as **supplementary dynamic MRI teaching media** with full-montage playback, pause/seek controls, original-download access and source credits. Any poster should retain all seven views. No autoplay, looping or phase annotation is needed for fidelity. The movie should not be labelled Radiography/VFSS, used to establish a normal report, fill native 3D geometry requirements or serve as independent aspiration validation. Anatomical leaf coverage remains pending. Browser decode/playback and any actual packaging remain to be verified by the integration work.
