# Published vascular-complication examples for acute pancreatitis

Added **three original CT source figures** to the local acute-pancreatitis reader, complementing its collection examples. They retain original arrows, image bytes, article identities and source context. These are local teaching examples, not complete acquired vascular studies or independently approved reporting structures.

Each article XML and image file matched its publisher MD5 from the NLM/PMC Article Dataset. Article/figure DOI identities, copyright, complete original captions, exact version-specific grants, byte hashes and dimensions are retained in the [source review](pancreatitis-vascular-source-review/original-source-review.json). Selected complete captions/figure records were inspected for excluded rights notices; no figure-specific restrictive credit was found. The noncommercial PSMVT study PMC6148429 was not substituted as a commercially reusable image source.

The 2025 GDA article body says an urgent CT angiogram demonstrated the abnormality in Figure 2. Its legend instead describes a coronal angiographic image during an interventional procedure. Direct image inspection shows CT anatomical appearance. Both original statements remain recorded; the reader identifies the context discrepancy rather than silently rewriting the legend or importing the image as catheter angiography. The body also reports bleeding, but this single still is not independent verification of active extravasation, source phase timing, arterial-neck dimensions or a treatment plan. These are explicitly held claims.

The 2022 source has an explicit CC BY 3.0 XML grant, while a newer publisher PDF advertises CC BY 4.0. The selected original repository image retains its original XML's 3.0 grant; neither the global publisher policy nor another distribution is used to relabel it. Its recurrent-pancreatitis setting remains explicit and is not asserted to be an acute-only source case. The portal-vein example does not establish complete portal/SMV/splenic-vein extent or calibrated thrombus burden.

All three images remain byte-for-byte original JPEGs. None is cropped, resized, sharpened, relabelled, re-annotated or reconstructed into a 3D volume. They are registered only for `ra.ct-pancreatitis`, with empty structure IDs/requirement coverage and pending anatomical review. No pseudoaneurysm wall/lumen/neck, bleeding extent, thrombus geometry or actual vessel course receives clinical-fidelity credit.
