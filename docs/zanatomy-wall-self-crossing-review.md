# Original oblique source-surface self-crossing review

The [complete interface audit](zanatomy-wall-interface-review.md) retained 24 nonordinary within-instance contacts. Reconstruction from the unchanged original native faces now classifies all 24 as **nonparallel triangle interior crossings**. They are not duplicate/coincident faces, ordinary shared-edge adjacency or a result of the fascia tessellation introduced in that audit: every affected native face is already an original triangle.

There are six findings each in right/left internal and external oblique. These repeat 12 unique geometry/face pairs through original right/left source transforms, rather than representing 24 independently observed patient-side defects. The contact-face graphs form two regions per instance: eight instance regions, four unique geometry regions. Original model IDs, face IDs, vertex IDs, material slots, world-coordinate triangles and contact endpoints remain attached to every finding.

All pairs share at most one native vertex, and their unit-normal cross norms exceed 0.17. Segment lengths range from approximately 0.015169 to 2.673490 mm in the original transformed artist coordinates. The minimum segment-midpoint barycentric weight is approximately 0.001211, putting the midpoint inside both original triangles; maximum reconstruction error is approximately 2.31445×10⁻¹³ mm. Each endpoint also reconstructs on both original face planes and within their triangle boundaries. These checks distinguish a three-dimensional interior crossing from a projection overlap or permitted contact only at a shared vertex. They do not establish acquired anatomical resolution, clinical measurement precision or biological tissue collision.

Each pair uses the same original material slot on both faces. Internal-oblique pairs carry the artist label `External rotation`; external-oblique pairs carry `Internal rotator`. None of these 24 pairs is an independently validated muscle/tendon-material interface. Material labels remain artist metadata, not histological identity. The existing native edge/winding findings and anatomical accuracy limitations remain separate concerns.
