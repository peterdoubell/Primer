# Corrected liver MRI source acquisition and reading review

LiverHccSeg version 1.1, [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.8179129](https://zenodo.org/records/8179129), is now under source review for MRI-specific liver requirements. The corrected version explicitly replaces an incorrect liver mask; older version 1.0 files must not be substituted. Zenodo publishes the derived dataset under CC BY 4.0, while the original [TCGA-LIHC image source](https://www.cancerimagingarchive.net/collection/tcga-lihc/) identifies CC BY 3.0. Original image and added-annotation attribution/versions remain separate. No source licence is silently relabelled as a model or code grant.

The source describes named original precontrast, arterial, portal-venous and delayed T1-weighted volumes, plus separately named derivatives registered into arterial image space. The latter involve nonrigid B-spline registration; they are not original acquired grids or measured finer anatomy. Two raters' liver/tumour masks are supplied separately. This work preserves both instead of creating an unreviewed average or consensus boundary.

The small clinical/reading metadata workbook passed its publisher MD5 and size checks. Its table has a repeated lower header with omitted tumour-specific columns. The parser now records that section structure explicitly and retains 19 source readings for 17 patients, rather than treating a header as a patient or manufacturing missing tumour values. Source comments include treatment/no-residual-tumour context, poor quality and incomplete-liver acquisition. Those conditions remain source-review requirements, not automatically eligible untreated observations or normal anatomy.

The metadata's tumour-size values are retained without assuming their units. Contrast-agent type is not treated as proof that a hepatobiliary phase was acquired; original DICOM sequence identity and timing must be checked. T2, diffusion/ADC, fat-sensitive and hepatobiliary requirements receive no automatic coverage from a contrast-phase T1 collection. Dataset categories remain source readings rather than recomputed diagnoses or clinical eligibility decisions.

Full corrected NIfTI/mask and original DICOM archive acquisition was started with streaming publisher-size and MD5 verification. The process was confirmed live by its exec handle, and the first archive was observed past 384 MB. Neither full archive is yet verified at this checkpoint. No partial file is treated as complete or extracted as trusted data. `source-acquisition-handoff.json` identifies the current handle and expected publisher hashes; the existing process must be re-polled or its writer inspected before any restart. No observation file or timeout alone establishes completion.

Three parser regression checks passed, and 20 related liver/fidelity checks passed in the application environment. They preserve repeated-header structure, source treatment/quality comments and unknown clinical eligibility. No source workbook was modified, no masks were combined, and no MRI image, model or requirement binding was promoted.

Next work is to finish the confirmed acquisition, verify publisher whole-archive integrity, inventory original versus registered volumes and both raters, and inspect actual native sequence/case geometry. Source sampling, phase/agent identity, registration validity, complete anatomy and diagnostic context remain unproven. The full objective remains all radiology modules; the known floor is 10,509 obligations and the complete denominator remains unknown. This source work is local and undeployed, with the acquisition still in progress.

Original source case TCGA-BC-A3KG now has a separate native-plane review for 151 DICOM objects from series 2, 3, 4, 6 and 11. Every selected member passed ZIP CRC and received a SHA-256 identity after whole-archive publisher MD5 verification. No source pixels were modified, no resampling was performed and no arterial mask was transferred.

Series 3 contains two echoes (2.38 and 4.87 ms at declared 1.5 T), each with 30 planes. Their declared position, orientation, pixel spacing, slice thickness and frame-of-reference match exactly, permitting paired native display. Each echo retains approximately 8.75 mm centre spacing and 7 mm source slice thickness; the missing interslice extent is not acquired anatomy. The two echoes must never be interleaved as a fictitious denser stack. Geometry agreement does not establish motion-free correspondence, a quantitative fat fraction or an approved pathological signal interpretation.

Its 30 planes share declared geometry with series 2, but none are pixel-identical. Acquisition times differ. This conflict is retained for source review; series 11 is not renamed, deduplicated or credited as T1. The single series-6 oblique plane has 50 mm thickness and TE 746 ms; it cannot provide a fine 3D boundary.

Three source figures show paired echoes, separate spin-echo acquisitions and the original thick slab. Source row/column directions and physical distances are explicit. The initial thick-slab title was clipped during render review; it was fixed and the final figure inspected. Source masks and registered derivatives are absent from these displays. Eight geometry tests passed, including repeated-plane rejection, exact echo pairing, frame/geometry mismatch rejection, preservation of irregular sampling and single-slab limits. The 23 related liver/fidelity checks also passed.

This review advances original MRI source verification. It grants no ADC/diffusion, hepatobiliary phase, clinical eligibility, complete anatomical boundary or model coverage. These additions remain local review evidence; PR #63's production release precedes them.

## Independent original MRI rater surfaces

Four separate unsmoothed Marching Cubes surfaces now retain the original rater-1 and rater-2 liver and tumour-1 masks in declared RAS millimetres. Every source component is retained; there is no boundary average, pruning, hole filling, decimation or fitted coordinate transform. Mesh arrays remain in ignored research storage, with hashes, audit receipts and two all-triangle review figures preserved here. Sampling remains 1.40625 × 1.40625 × 2.5 mm; an interpolated surface does not measure a finer anatomical boundary.

The surfaces contain 91,794 / 90,388 liver triangles and 26,048 / 25,948 tumour triangles. Full native-plane interior comparisons tested 2,695,392 voxel centres, including all 857,219 source positive voxels across the four masks, with zero false positives or negatives. All surfaces have closed, consistently oriented edges, cyclic vertex links and positive-area triangles. Complete self-contact checks considered 1,889,115 conservative candidate triangle pairs, with zero unexpected contacts. These tests validate numerical source export, not the raters' anatomical accuracy.

Rater-1 liver has surface Euler characteristic −2 (genus 2); rater-2 liver has Euler 0 (genus 1). Both original liver masks have voxel Euler characteristic 0 under 6- and 26-connectivity. Those discrete and continuous definitions are not silently equated. Both liver surfaces remain held for source topology/boundary review; no handle was filled to make the result look anatomically smoother. Both tumour surfaces have Euler 2 (genus 0), and their source masks have voxel Euler 1 under both adjacencies. A simple surface is not clinical truth.

All 67,428 rater-1 tumour voxels lie within that rater's liver mask. Rater 2 contains 1,884 of 66,105 tumour voxels outside its own liver mask. This is an annotation relation, not evidence of invasion, an exophytic boundary or a reason to modify either source mask. Between liver/tumour surface interactions, complete original MRI boundary assessment and the source convention require further review. Both raters remain independent.

Three source-extent regression checks passed: preservation of multiple source components, rejection of a pruned component and detection of a physically shifted mesh. Twenty related liver/fidelity checks passed. Both 3D figure views were inspected with explicit RAS axes and a common physical scale. No model was promoted, no coverage was granted to capsule, segmental vessels, bile ducts or other lesions, and clinical readiness remains unproven.
