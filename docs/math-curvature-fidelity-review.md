# Surface curvature and octant transport: fidelity review

The dedicated `math.5.diffgeo.curvature-transport` activity compares the Euclidean plane, a circular cylinder, the round sphere and the standard smooth ring torus with `R > r > 0`. Its embedded surface metric, shape operator and integrals are analytic. The filled drawings are finite planar facets of the stated parameterizations; pixels and facet areas do not determine the calculations. The sphere transport remains a separate spherical example for every surface selection. The existing sphere triangle activity is preserved.

## Mathematical derivation and scope

Let `u` be longitude (a length coordinate on the plane) and `v` latitude or tube angle (a length coordinate on the plane and cylinder). With `s = ±1` selecting the outward or reversed unit normal, the first form is `I = E du² + 2F du dv + G dv²`. The second form is `II = e du² + 2f du dv + g dv²`, where `e = n·X_uu`, `f = n·X_uv`, `g = n·X_vv`. The convention is `S = −Dn`, so on a regular chart `S = I⁻¹II`. All four coordinate systems are orthogonal and diagonalize `S`.

| Surface embedding `X(u,v)` | `E, F, G` | `e, f, g` | Principal curvatures | `dA` coefficient | Gaussian `K` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `(u,v,0)` | `1,0,1` | `0,0,0` | `0,0` | `1` | `0` |
| `(r cos u,r sin u,v)` | `r²,0,1` | `−sr,0,0` | `−s/r,0` | `r` | `0` |
| `r(cos v cos u,cos v sin u,sin v)` | `r² cos²v,0,r²` | `−sr cos²v,0,−sr` | `−s/r,−s/r` | `r² cos v` | `1/r²` |
| `((R+r cos v) cos u,(R+r cos v) sin u,r sin v)` | `(R+r cos v)²,0,r²` | `−s(R+r cos v)cos v,0,−sr` | `−s cos v/(R+r cos v),−s/r` | `r(R+r cos v)` | `cos v/[r(R+r cos v)]` |

Differentiating each embedding and taking tangent-vector dot products gives the first form. Taking the Hessian dot products with the chosen unit normal gives the second form. Their diagonal ratios give the principal curvatures, and their product gives `K`. Reversing the normal changes `II` and both principal signs; it preserves the metric, area and `K`.

The cylinder unrolls under `x = r u`, `z = v` to `dx² + dz²`: it is locally intrinsically flat despite one nonzero extrinsic principal curvature. The embedded torus is not the abstract flat quotient of a Euclidean rectangle. Its outer region has positive `K`, its inner region negative `K`, and its top and bottom rings have zero `K`. [Oregon State's torus calculation](https://sites.science.oregonstate.edu/coursewikis/GDF/book/gdf/torus.html) supplies the induced metric and intrinsic curvature; its signed-principal discussion uses a different convention, so the activity explicitly derives the signs for `S = −Dn`.

For a rectangle `[u₀,u₁] × [v₀,v₁]`, set `Δu = u₁−u₀`, `Δv = v₁−v₀` and `Δsin = sin v₁−sin v₀`. Plane and cylinder curvature integrals vanish. On the sphere, the area is `r² Δu Δsin` and the curvature integral is `Δu Δsin`. On the torus they are respectively `r Δu(R Δv + r Δsin)` and `Δu Δsin`. Torus positive and negative contributions are separately integrated after splitting the interval at `v = π/2 + kπ`. For the full torus they are exactly `+4π` and `−4π`, with total zero and absolute integral `8π`. The sphere total is `4π` while its area is `4πr²`. These agree with [closed-surface Gauss–Bonnet](https://sites.science.oregonstate.edu/coursewikis/GDF/book/gdf/gaussbonnet.html); the open plane and cylinder patches make no closed-surface claim.

Only exact represented quarter-turn arguments are recognized as symbolic zeros. Nearby values retain their metric, Jacobian and tangent directions. Patch integrals use a stable sine-difference identity with distances from the nearby quarter turn; this keeps even one-representable-step pole patches positive. Known exact quarter-turn endpoints retain exact sine differences, including zero at the complete torus boundaries `−π,+π`. The zero vector has no measured transport angle even though the loop's theoretical holonomy is known.

At sphere poles, `E` and the chart area coefficient vanish, and the chart has no inverse metric or longitude tangent direction. The surface remains smooth with `K = 1/r²`. Longitude seams identify points and frames rather than creating holes. A single global chart for the compact sphere or torus would have compact image under a homeomorphism, while a nonempty open subset of `R²` is not compact.

For transport, let the unit vertex directions be `A=(1,0,0)`, `B=(0,1,0)`, `C=(0,0,1)`, with physical vertices `rA,rB,rC`. Initial `β` is measured in the fixed physical basis `(+Y,+Z)` at `rA` and is independent of the chosen normal. Each edge is a great-circle quarter arc. Rotating both its radius and tangent vector about the edge's fixed axis preserves tangency and norm. If `n` is the unit radius, `a` the edge axis and `V` tangent, then `a·n=0` and `V ∈ span(a,a×n)`, so `dV/dθ = a×V` is normal to the sphere and its tangent covariant derivative is zero. At each corner the same physical vector enters the next leg without an imposed turn. The positive path returns `(0,y,z)` as `(0,−z,y)`, a `+π/2` angle around outward `A`; reverse traversal gives `−π/2`. Reversing the measurement normal reverses the signed angle without changing input, path or physical vectors. The octant area is `πr²/2` and `∫K dA=π/2`. [Oregon State's geodesic-triangle derivation](https://sites.science.oregonstate.edu/coursewikis/GDF/book/gdf/geotri.html) gives the same excess from three right angles.

These are Riemannian surfaces in Euclidean three-space. They do not implement Lorentzian spacetime, Weyl curvature, tidal tensors, geodesic deviation or arbitrary-surface transport. No finite preview proves the general theorem or completes the wider mathematics/physics/radiology objective.
