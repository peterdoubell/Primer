# Abdominal-trauma CT fidelity scope — 2026-10-05

The abdominal-trauma reporting investigation now defines 690 draft targets and 2,070 clinical-image, schematic and 3D obligations. The inventory follows the actual five reporting fields: solid organs, bleeding, bowel/mesentery, urinary tract and associated injury. It separately names hepatic segments and interfaces, splenic regions, bilateral renal/adrenal/ureter structures, pancreatic regions and duct interfaces, biliary anatomy, actual vessel branches/confluences, peritoneal/retroperitoneal compartments, bowel segments, leak sites, bladder protocol/distension, bilateral diaphragm regions, covered skeletal structures, penetrating tracks and grading/acquisition evidence. Every actual case-specific site, branch, margin and host interface still requires instantiation and independent review. Conditional anatomy requires actual coverage and relevance; the draft does not waive unseen tissues or grant complete source anatomy from a parent label.

The report presets now retain adequate, limited and unassessed alternatives instead of pre-filling intact organs, complete bleeding exclusion, no bowel injury or an intact diaphragm. Actual arterial, venous, delayed and baseline sources, timing, contrast/opacification and field/artefacts remain distinct. Phase-dependent bleeding interpretation needs corresponding sites and blood-pool context. Single-phase appearance, absent apparent growth, a still or schematic colour cannot independently establish containment, viability or complete injury exclusion.

Early injury and unresolved bowel, mesenteric and pancreatic duct interfaces remain explicit. Actual excretory collecting-system assessment is separate from retrograde bladder cystography; the latter retains adequate, limited and not-performed choices and actual distension/source limits. Partial tracks cannot establish all crossings or entry/exit endpoints. Dense clot can suggest a source but does not independently establish every bleeding site. Immediate communication of consequential injury is retained; no new trauma-treatment algorithm or repeat-scan timing rule is introduced.

The generic 2018 grade example has been replaced by the actual organ-specific scale/revision and observed criterion evidence. The [AAST official revision resource](https://www.aast.org/resources/trauma-tools/organ-injury-scale.html) was checked and lists renal 2025 and pancreatic 2024 revisions alongside the earlier 2018 spleen/liver/kidney reference. This does not establish one universal year, copy full grading tables, assign a grade without its evidence, or declare older classifications invalid in their stated context. The full lesson, quiz, scoring and organ-specific grade/management reconciliation remains pending.

Primary sources checked on 2026-10-05: [Radiology Assistant abdominal trauma](https://radiologyassistant.nl/abdomen/acute-abdomen/ct-in-trauma), the above AAST resource, [WSES bowel injury 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-022-00418-y), [WSES-AAST duodeno-pancreatic/biliary trauma 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0278-6), and [EAU urological trauma](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/urological-trauma/chapter/urogenital-trauma-guidelines). The bowel and pancreatic sources support retaining early/limited diagnostic uncertainty. EAU distinguishes actual retrograde cystography from passive excretory bladder filling. Only the focused acquisition/interpretation limits and source references are introduced here; numerical injury/grade thresholds, complete management guidance and licensed table reproduction remain outside this change.
