What sets a galaxy’s circular speed?

Ideal circular orbits in a stationary, axisymmetric disk plus a spherical halo. This is a hypothetical model, not measured galaxy data.

Gold: stellar disk · Blue: halo · White: combined circular speed · Purple points: fixed synthetic sample with ±1σ bars.

Disk density in three dimensions

Surface at 10% of the current central disk density. It is a density contour, not the edge of the disk. Equal scale on every axis; rotation changes only the view. The orthographic scale bar measures distances parallel to the view plane.

The comparison sample is generated from one fixed disk/halo model with a reproducible pseudo-random Gaussian noise realization (σ = 8 km/s at each radius). It stays fixed as you change the model. Error bars are one assumed standard deviation, not confidence intervals for fitted masses. The mismatch is Σ[(sample − model)/σ]²; no p-value or unique physical conclusion is inferred.

The disk uses a Miyamoto–Nagai potential; its midplane force cannot be calculated by treating its mass as spherical. The halo follows an NFW density profile. Halo scale mass is 4πρₛrₛ³, not total or virial mass. The untruncated halo’s total mass diverges; this view covers only 0–40 kpc. Bars, spiral structure, gas pressure and non-circular motions are omitted. A rotation curve alone does not uniquely establish dark-matter physics.
