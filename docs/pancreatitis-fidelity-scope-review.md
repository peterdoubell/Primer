# Acute pancreatitis reporting fidelity scope

Expanded `ra.ct-pancreatitis` / `rad.5.pancreas-acute` to **258 reporting targets and 774 image/schematic/model obligations**. Each prototype requires actual source-case instantiation and specialist review. This inventory expands the requirements; it does not claim new anatomical coverage or approve existing source models.

The scope includes whole acquired pancreatic regions and margins; actual enhancement, pancreatic/peripancreatic necrosis and viable interfaces; every acquired spread compartment; every collection's complete extent, contents, debris, wall, locules, gas and source time-point correspondence; APFC/ANC/pseudocyst/WON case examples; venous courses, lumen/wall, occlusion and collateral routes; arterial courses/branches, pseudoaneurysm and bleeding interfaces; main/accessory duct and outflow, disruption clues and viable upstream gland; biliary stone/obstruction anatomy; adjacent bowel, fistula/perfusion/perforation context; serosal/distant effects; actual intervention/altered anatomy; and conditionally acquired MRI/ultrasound evidence. CT, normal atlas geometry or a parent-organ mesh cannot supply positive disease, unacquired sequences or fine wall/lumen targets.

Clinical severity, infection, onset, intervention urgency and biological interpretation remain functional evidence requirements. They cannot be inferred from model shapes, collection age or a CT severity score. Source and diagnostic uncertainty must remain explicit, and the inventory is not a treatment algorithm.

Corrected several concrete teaching shortcuts:

- The day-26 incomplete-wall question now asks for observed maturity rather than a fixed two-day wait. The overview and reporting walkthrough retain content and actual encapsulation; calendar age alone does not confer maturity.
- The fluid-responsive hypotension question now grades the stated local complication without inventing qualifying persistent organ failure. Its explanation requires actual clinical criteria and duration.
- The early necrotic-collection question retains anatomical classification and directs intervention decisions to multidisciplinary assessment rather than an automatic wait-unless-infection rule.
- The infection-suspicion question retains instrumentation/fistula context and removes automatic routine CT-guided aspiration. Gas is a finding requiring clinical interpretation, not standalone microbiological proof.
- The reporting walkthrough qualifies gallstones/duct dilatation as cause/obstruction concern instead of treating those findings alone as established biliary aetiology.

Sources are retained in the effective reporting guide: [Revised Atlanta consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23100216/), [2016 clinical practice guideline — severity definitions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4814287/), [AGA pancreatic-necrosis guidance](https://gastro.org/clinical-guidance/management-of-pancreatic-necrosis/) and [Radiology Assistant acute-pancreatitis reporting scope](https://radiologyassistant.nl/abdomen/pancreas/acute-pancreatitis). The original consensus publisher's full text/PDF returned access errors during this review; accessible primary abstract/guideline evidence and the reporting source were used, and no newly read full consensus table is claimed. Historical guideline sections are not labelled a complete current management algorithm.

The broader lesson still contains additional management questions and clinical claims requiring separate source reconciliation. That hold is explicit in the scope record. The current work does not establish a complete current intervention guideline, complete lesson review, independent clinical approval or certification readiness.
