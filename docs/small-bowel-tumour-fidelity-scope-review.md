# Small-bowel tumour CT fidelity scope review — 2026-10-04

The small-bowel tumour investigation now defines 329 draft targets and 987 image, schematic and model obligations. Individually covered duodenal/jejunal/ileal regions and the ileocaecal interface remain distinct from every actual lesion, component, margin, growth pattern, luminal effect, mesenteric/nodal observation, vessel branch/interface and spread site. Conditional periampullary, adjacent-organ and postoperative/treatment regions require actual coverage and clinical relevance. Specific positive tumour and mimic examples cannot borrow generic normal bowel coverage.

The inventory requires complete source-specific primary/mesenteric/nodal identities and extent. Orthogonal measurements and bowel length do not combine separate observations into one tumour. Endoluminal, mural and exophytic components, internal low-attenuation regions, ulcer/communication observations, host interfaces, intussusception anatomy and residual vascular lumen require their own source evidence. Visible CT bands do not manufacture histological wall layers, and a parent bowel model cannot approve the lesion or its interfaces.

The reader now retains actual bowel coverage/distension, acquired contrast phases, suitable reference attenuation and opacification limits. Negative presets no longer create blanket absence of mass, mesenteric disease or metastases when assessment is incomplete. Histology, molecular/mitotic grade, GIST biological risk, microscopic invasion and operative suitability remain outside pattern-only CT interpretation. Source low-attenuation regions are described with their uncertainty rather than asserted as histological necrosis. Actual liver/peritoneal and other covered-site observations do not exclude occult or microscopic spread.

Primary sources checked: [Lee et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.3348/jksr.2021.0168), [Brogna et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare13091071), and the existing [Radiology Assistant source](https://radiologyassistant.nl/abdomen/bowel/small-bowel-tumors). Publisher XML for both journal reviews was retrieved from the NLM/PMC Article Dataset and verified against its supplied MD5. They document overlapping and atypical CT presentations, including similarly enhancing small lesions and tumour/mimic overlap. This focused qualification does not introduce a new staging, risk-classification, treatment or universal enterography protocol.

Relevant suspected acute complications retain urgent communication in their clinical context. No inferred tumour diagnosis, histological grade, operative verdict or microscopic spread decision is generated from a model colour, phase-unqualified appearance or vessel contact. The whole shared tumour/foreign-body lesson, quiz, staging and management content remains independently unreconciled. Source-image rights are separate from the reporting source review: the 2023 article's noncommercial grant is not treated as clearance for commercial image redistribution; no new image is promoted in this turn.
