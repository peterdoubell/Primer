# MSK image source, depiction and reuse audit

Audit date: 26 September 2026. Scope: `data/radiology/detailed-visuals.json`, `anatomical-illustrations.json`, `investigation-source-images.json`, `web/reference-media/`, related local review documents, and primary publisher/licensor pages. This records source and visual evidence, not independent specialist validation of every clinical assertion.

## Findings that affect the release

The existing detailed MSK gallery contains useful actual clinical images and authored anatomical diagrams, but neither full anatomical coverage nor commercial reuse authorization is demonstrated by its metadata. Every one of its 76 figures is hosted by The Radiology Assistant; none has a license or permission record. Attribution and an image URL do not establish a commercial redistribution grant. The generated shoulder bone illustration is not evidence for fine anatomy, even though its reference meshes have a reuse license.

Twelve original, openly licensed figures were acquired in two bounded batches during this audit. Their pixels, labels and panels are preserved. They are recorded in `data/radiology/msk-open-images.json` and saved under `web/reference-media/msk-open/`; the adjacent `ATTRIBUTION.md` preserves exact captions and acquisition details. These figures cover selected structures in six investigations. They must not be counted as a complete MSK atlas.

## Publisher permissions: established facts and limits

Later clarification on the same audit date: the [website disclaimer](https://radiologyassistant.nl/disclaimer)
expressly permits educational reproduction with credit to the original author
and Radiology Assistant. This is an existing reuse basis, not an implicit
noncommercial restriction. Commercial web-app hosting, adaptations and
item-specific third-party scope remain under reconciliation; this audit must
not be read as claiming that all web reproduction lacks permission.

The [Radiology Assistant information page](https://radiologyassistant.nl/more/radiology-assistant/information) and reviewed [shoulder anatomy article](https://radiologyassistant.nl/musculoskeletal/shoulder/mri-anatomy) did not present an open commercial reuse license. Its [official app page](https://radiologyassistant.nl/more/apps/radiology-assistant-2-0-app) identifies the related app offering. The [Radiology Assistant 2.0 legal document](https://doradiology.com/RA2Legal.pdf), page 1, says that article-content copyright belongs to RadiologyAssistant.nl, requires prior written permission for content reuse and expressly prohibits commercial use under that app agreement. Its teaching exception concerns unpublished personal slides/direct classroom teaching.

That PDF is an **app agreement**, not proof that identical contractual terms bind every web visitor. The defensible audit status for the web gallery is **commercial permission not established**; it is not an assertion that a specific web use has already been adjudicated unlawful. Remote hosting does not create a permission record. Keep publisher article links as references while obtaining a written grant covering the planned product, or substitute figures with a documented compatible license. Do not silently download and republish the existing publisher catalog.

[CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) explicitly permits commercial copying and adaptation with attribution, a license link and change notice. Preserve any figure-specific exclusions. Share-alike licenses also require the applicable adaptation terms to be met; a public webpage, a search result, or the word “open” alone is not enough.

## Actions in order

1. **Separate rights clearance from anatomical review.** Mark existing Radiology Assistant figures as external references with commercial permission unresolved until a permission document exists. The absence of local hosting does not settle reuse rights.
2. **Bind the twelve new assets narrowly.** Use each manifest's `structures_visible`, exact modality/plane caption and `limits`. A figure counts only for structures it actually depicts at sufficient resolution. Do not count neighboring bones or a general topic label as all-structure coverage.
3. **Build a per-structure coverage ledger.** For each reportable structure, track normal clinical example, abnormal example when relevant, schematic, 3D geometry, source/rights record and clinical reviewer. Use explicit statuses such as missing, source-acquired, depiction-reviewed and specialist-validated; missing material must stay missing rather than fall back to generic imagery.
4. **Commission or obtain missing primary anatomy.** Prioritize shoulder pulley/cuff footprints, knee roots and PLC structures, wrist TFCC subdivisions/intrinsic and extrinsic ligaments, and ankle deltoid/spring/syndesmotic components. Acquire consistent multiplanar, de-identified examinations and approved annotations where publications cannot supply them. Do not synthesize diagnostic signal or anatomical structures with image generation.
5. **Review clinical and product claims separately.** A named MSK radiologist should verify normal variants, planes, labels, attachments and pathology correspondence against primary images. An intact mesh, a valid license, successful rendering or an attractive photograph is insufficient evidence for “all reportable structures” or clinical/commercial-grade completeness.
