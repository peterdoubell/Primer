# Two drafts of the Gettysburg Address

The activity pairs the complete Library of Congress exhibition transcriptions with the four manuscript pages shown in that exhibition. It serves the photographs at their original exhibition dimensions. The WebP renditions preserve every decoded RGB pixel from the exhibition JPEGs. Lincoln’s 1863 manuscripts are in the public domain. Credit for the manuscript images and transcriptions is given to the Library of Congress, Manuscript Division. [Exhibition](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/gettysburg-address/exhibition-items.html) · [Nicolay transcription](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/gettysburg-address/ext/trans-nicolay-copy.html) · [Hay transcription](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/gettysburg-address/ext/trans-hay-draft.html)

The twelve passages cover both full transcriptions. Paragraph breaks follow the exhibition. Selecting a passage also selects the bands of handwriting that contain it, including both pages when a reading crosses the page break. Three additional image details show corrections in the Hay manuscript. These bands and details were checked against the four photographs.

The comparison tokenizes each transcription, optionally including punctuation and distinguishing upper and lower case. A longest-common-subsequence alignment preserves token order. Highlighting and the variant table use the original transcription offsets. An unmatched token describes the chosen alignment; repeated words sometimes permit several equally long alignments. The activity makes no reconstruction of the speech as delivered.

The source receipt records the four original image URLs and hashes, both transcript URLs and hashes, and the dimensions, encoded hashes and decoded RGB hashes of the displayed pages.
