# Acute-abdomen modality and anatomy scope — 2026-10-05

The acute-abdomen investigation now defines 724 draft targets and 2,172 clinical-image, schematic and model obligations. Actual pain regions, bowel/appendiceal and diverticular structures, hepatic segments, hepatobiliary/pancreatic/splenic interfaces, bilateral renal/ureter/adrenal regions, relevant pelvic organs, compartments, collections, vessel branches and hernia/wall interfaces are separately retained. Every case-specific site, margin, branch and host interface still requires instantiation and independent review. Relevant pelvic, adrenal, wall or postoperative structures are conditional on actual anatomy, coverage and clinical context, not waived as absent.

The catalogue is multimodality. Cross-sectional anatomical targets admit actual CT, ultrasound or MRI sources where their required structures are resolved; radiography has its own projection/gas/radiopaque-object/bone/field targets and cannot supply fine appendiceal wall or resolved organ anatomy. CT technical references remain separate from positive CT abnormal-enhancement/fat/attenuation examples. Ultrasound dynamic/compression/tenderness/Murphy/Doppler sources and settings are separate from CT attenuation/phase and MRI sequence/signal/diffusion correspondence. Static images, other modalities or source colours cannot supply these missing features.

The five reporting presets retain adequate, limited and unassessed alternatives. Appendix visualisation is explicit: seen, partly seen, not seen or not assessed. Nonvisualisation is not automatically a normal appendix. Actual CT/US/MRI findings precede interpretation; sonographic compression/Murphy findings require actual assessment. Wall/fat/nodal patterns do not independently identify infection or a pathogen. Solid-organ, urinary/pelvic and peritoneal/vascular fields retain their actual coverage, source and unresolved extent instead of blanket negative findings.

Pain location organises source assessment but does not independently establish the cause or exclude other acute processes. Actual phase, sequence, field, patient and source-timing limits remain necessary for organ, vessel, urinary or complication observations. Free gas/fluid or a coloured schematic does not establish complete injury, perfusion or viability. Existing prompt communication of consequential findings is retained. No new quantitative diagnostic cutoff, management timing rule or universal imaging algorithm is introduced.

That scope is not applied as a universal rule.
