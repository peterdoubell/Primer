# Ultrasound IBD fidelity scope — 2026-10-05

The intestinal-ultrasound reporting investigation now defines 355 draft targets and 1,065 image/schematic/model obligations. Actual assessed bowel segments, acoustic wall bands, caliper sites, Doppler sources, dynamic observations, narrowings, tract courses/branches/endpoints, collections, mesenteric/node regions, postoperative anatomy, relevant host/mimic sites and corresponding prior/current sources are separate targets. Every source-specific site and interface still requires explicit instantiation and independent anatomical/clinical review.

The scope follows the actual five reporting fields. Accessible duodenal regions, deep pelvic/rectal regions and adjacent organs are conditional on actual coverage and relevance. Unseen segments cannot become an examined normal survey. The five sonographic bands are acoustic interfaces; this draft does not equate them with independently verified histological layers. Loss of visible stratification is retained as an observation, rather than proof of histological transmural inflammation.

Doppler requires actual acquired settings and mural/perimural source observations with artefact and technical limits. Missing or inadequate colour cannot exclude activity or establish absent perfusion/viability. Peristalsis, compressibility and persistent narrowing require real-time, cine or reliable repeated-source evidence; published stills and static 3D models cannot supply these features. MRI, CT or a different patient's panels cannot substitute for ultrasound findings.

The reporting presets now preserve adequate, limited and unassessed coverage/wall/Doppler/dynamic/complication alternatives. Comparison preserves matched, limited and unavailable choices, replacing assumed unchanged disease. Actual source-visible tract endpoints, inaccessible deep/pelvic extent and uncertain host interfaces remain explicit. Routine transabdominal ultrasound is not a complete dedicated perianal examination. Wall, fat, nodes and ulcer-like patterns do not independently identify a pathogen, quantify fibrosis or establish remission. Suspicion of obstruction, abscess or perforation is escalated in the appropriate clinical context; technical limitations do not produce a blanket exclusion.
