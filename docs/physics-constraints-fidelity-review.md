# Classical constraints, momentum and laboratory work — review, 2026-10-11

The new `physics-constraints-lab` activity addresses the assessed `phys.4.classical` examples with SI quantities and explicit ideal-constraint assumptions. It preserves the existing fixed-endpoint free-particle action experiment and separate oscillator inset in `HamiltonianMotionLab`; their action and energy represent different systems. The new rotating-hoop activity distinguishes the reduced autonomous Hamiltonian from laboratory mechanical energy, and compares zero allowed virtual work with actual motor work. This review does not close the broader physics, mathematics or clinical source-fidelity work.

The renderer accepts only `{scenario:'phys.4.classical.constraints-energy'}` on this lesson. Python tests reject extra props, an unrelated scenario and use on another lesson; the shell loads the helper and CSS before the registry. The public CommonJS/browser API includes `build`, `render`, `hoopSnapshot`, `doubleSnapshot`, analytic motion helpers and numerical integration helpers. State fields are mass/length/stiffness/gravity, initial coordinates and velocities, constant drive speed, model choice and inspection time. Lengths and masses remain positive; the elapsed-time window is 0–4 seconds. Angles in inputs are degrees; helper snapshots use radians. Displayed momentum, force, power and energy retain their own units.

| --- | --- |
| Upward free fall; fixed incline; free-particle Hamiltonian | Analytic `q=q₀+v₀t−at²/2`, `v=v₀−at`, with downward `a=g` or `g sinα`; `H=½mv²+mgq` or `½mv²+mgq sinα`. At 30° and 9.8 m/s², downhill acceleration is 4.9 m/s². A 0.5 kg free mass at 4 m/s has H=4 J. No ground/track endpoint is introduced. |
| Spring Lagrangian versus Hamiltonian | Analytic harmonic motion with arbitrary initial position/velocity; `T=½mv²`, `V=½kq²`, `L=T−V`, `p=mv`, `H=p²/(2m)+V`. L varies while H is constant. |
| Simple pendulum and the small-angle limit | Nonlinear `θ̈=−(g/l)sinθ` and the finite-energy elliptic-integral libration period. At l=2.45 m, g=9.8 m/s², the small-angle frequency is 2 rad/s. The small-angle approximation is not substituted into the nonlinear curve. A rigid rod can push/pull; the model explicitly does not silently replace it with a string at slackening/rotation states. |
| Cyclic coordinate and central force | Analytic planar motion in the selected isotropic spring potential. `pφ=m(xv_y−yv_x)=mr²φ̇` is conserved. The special closed orbit does not establish closure for arbitrary central potentials. Cartesian coordinates remain valid where the polar chart becomes singular. |
| Fixed wire and generalized constraints | Angular coordinates enforce fixed lengths in the geometry; the ledger retains the canonical momentum and energy. Ideal reactions are eliminated from the reduced equations, rather than claimed absent from Newton's laws. |
| Double pendulum | Two independent angles from four planar coordinates minus two fixed lengths. Cartesian kinetic energy supplies the coupled positive mass matrix; `p=M(θ)θ̇` and its generally nonzero rates are shown. Ideal rods have zero total power; an internal rod may transfer energy between the individual bobs. |
| Prescribed rotating hoop | Full laboratory embedding, velocity, acceleration, gravity, reaction, azimuthal momentum, torque/power, reduced H, laboratory E and independently integrated motor work. Ω is constant within each initial-value calculation. Editing Ω starts a different calculation, not an unmodelled acceleration protocol. |

The independent rotating-hoop derivation uses

`r = R(sinθ cosφ, sinθ sinφ, −cosθ)`, `φ=Ωt`, `V=mgR(1−cosθ)`.

Differentiating this Cartesian embedding gives `T=½mR²(θ̇²+Ω²sin²θ)` and `pθ=mR²θ̇`. The Legendre transform in the reduced θ coordinate gives

`Hred = ½mR²θ̇² − ½mR²Ω²sin²θ + V`,

`Elab = T+V = Hred + Ωpφ`, `pφ=mR²Ωsin²θ`.

The reaction from `ma−mg` satisfies `Rforce·∂r/∂θ=0`. Its actual power is `Rforce·v = Ω d(pφ)/dt = 2mR²Ω² sinθ cosθ θ̇`, which may be nonzero. Constant Ω conserves Hred, while laboratory E changes by motor work. At Ω=0 the laboratory and reduced energies coincide. The potential reference differs from Tong's by an additive constant, with no change in forces or conservation. These claims agree with [Tong, sections 2.3 and 2.5.1–2.5.2](https://davidtong.org/teaching/classical-dynamics/dynhtml/S2). The nonlinear and small-angle distinction follows [OpenStax's pendulum treatment](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/15-4-pendulums).

The trajectories use RK4 with actual step halving, up to eight refinements from h=1/256 s; states are never projected onto an energy surface or replaced by analytic endpoint snaps. Accepted curves require maximum sampled coarse/fine angle difference ≤2×10⁻⁶ rad and rate difference ≤2×10⁻⁵ rad/s, plus maximum conserved-energy and laboratory-energy/work residual over **every integration step** ≤`10⁻⁸ + 2×10⁻⁷ max(1,|H₀|,|E₀|)` J. Only 121 regular display samples plus the requested time are stored. A public helper guard limits integration to 300,000 steps and requires floating-point time to advance. The internal finest full-window calculation uses about 262,000 steps. Failure remains visible as a tentative curve; refinement differences and conservation diagnostics are not rigorous global error bounds.

A joint extreme double state (m₁=0.2 kg, m₂=3 kg, l₁=0.5 m, l₂=3 m, g=20 m/s², initial angles ±150°, rates ±3 rad/s) originally failed the four-refinement budget. Raising the ceiling without changing the domain or tolerances resolves it at h=1/32768 s: angle/rate differences 4.14×10⁻⁸ rad / 6.70×10⁻⁶ rad/s and maximum H drift 6.34×10⁻⁸ J. The final integration took roughly 80 ms locally. Some other joint corners use the eighth refinement. This is bounded-window evidence, not an accuracy theorem for every continuous parameter or an infinite-time/chaos claim.
