# WAW-TACE source and native-mask review

The independent [WAW-TACE dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/12741586) was checked as a potential liver phase/anatomy source. The current Zenodo record is open and lists CC BY 4.0. Its [author paper](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/ryai.240296) includes scientific-research-only data-sharing wording. Those statements are preserved separately; commercial-reader reuse remains uncleared pending reconciliation. No author contact, permission request or external message was sent.

The complete small metadata, tumour-mask and definitions releases passed publisher byte lengths and MD5 checks, with independent SHA-256 fingerprints. The original mask ZIP has 378 NRRD members, while the paper describes 377 lesion masks. Current file count is recorded without inventing a reason for that difference. All originals remain unchanged in ignored research storage.

The CT metadata contains 854 rows with numeric phase indices 0–3, filenames, tumour-count fields and per-study section thickness. Patient 10 has four phase-index rows with 2.5, 1.25, 2.5 and 2.5 mm thickness values. One tumour mask is present in its phase-index 2 entry. Zero mask counts on the other phase rows are not treated as proof of absent lesions. Exact CT/mask correspondence and explicit patient-level phase-name mapping remain to be verified; no phase names are assigned from filename convention alone.

Patient 10's original NRRD mask was CRC checked against its archive member and decoded without editing. It contains 141,622 positive voxels on a 512 × 512 × 172 grid, with declared LPS directions, approximately 0.703125 × 0.703125 × 2.5 mm sampling, and positive bounds matching the original header exactly. The NRRD uses generic Tissue terminology and an inprogress export tag; these remain source metadata rather than a diagnosis, failed final-review assumption or independent clinical approval.

The publisher distinguishes expert-corrected tumour annotations from automated organ masks. Its tumour workflow includes interpolation between outlined sections and 3 mm Gaussian smoothing. That processing must remain explicit in boundary-fidelity review; these labels do not measure a finer capsule or phase feature. Automated 104-organ outputs are not promoted as clinically verified branches, ducts, vessels or segment territories.

Selective reads of the large original CT ZIP were attempted using the existing strict source-range helper. Both official content and public-download URLs lacked its required stable identity tag, so the helper rejected them. No guard was weakened, no arbitrary archive offsets accepted and no original CT volume or publisher whole-CT-archive hash was acquired. A full acquisition or an independently supported source endpoint remains required before CT geometry and image/annotation correspondence can be checked. No CT acquisition process is left running.

Four analytical decoder checks passed: NRRD fast-axis ordering, declared direction/origin preservation, incomplete voxel-byte rejection, unsupported coordinate-space rejection and duplicate-header rejection. Publisher file integrity and actual decoded source bounds were independently checked. These are source-format results, not anatomical or diagnostic validation.

This source route remains a research candidate with unresolved reuse wording, CT access/integrity, mask/image correspondence, processed-boundary accuracy and clinical scope. No model, reader image, feature map or requirement binding was added. The full objective remains all radiology modules; the known floor stays 10,509, the complete denominator remains unknown and clinical/commercial readiness is unproven. The review is local and undeployed.
