# Shared biliary lesson: wall-pattern and clinical boundaries

The shared `rad.5.biliary` lesson previously said that comet-tail reverberation meant “adenomyomatosis, not cancer” and told the reader to “name it and stop.” That contradicted the newly acquired differential examples and the source reviews' discussion of uncertainty and coexisting carcinoma. It also supplied a generic no-cholecystectomy verdict for systemic wall oedema and lacked unassessed/not-applicable tenderness and unperformed Doppler choices in its template.

The reference approach, classification table, pitfalls, reporting template and associated quiz now preserve whole-lesion intramural spaces, layer/tissue integrity and hepatic interfaces. A suggestive artefact is not a universal cancer exclusion. Confidently characterized benign disease is distinguished from uncertain, symptomatic or suspicious/coexisting findings without introducing a new universal operative algorithm. Systemic oedema does not prove the absence of primary gallbladder disease. The wall threshold remains conventional measurement context, not a diagnostic or severity test.

The template explicitly retains actual/limited/unperformed position-change, tenderness and Doppler assessment. Twinkling artefact is separated from true flow. Fasting/repeat-examination advice no longer suggests delaying urgent assessment. Stricture length and shape remain observations with overlapping causes rather than a universal diagnostic cutoff.

These changes were checked against [Bonatti et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13244-017-0544-7) and [Riddell et al., published 2022 / issue 2023](https://doi.org/10.1259/bjr.20220115). Both source reviews are now delivered with the actual gallbladder reader references. Their scope and dates remain explicit; this is not a complete current polyp-management, surgical or endocrine guideline reconciliation.

The owned reasoning schematic was regenerated at both responsive sizes. It now asks for actual tenderness assessment separately, reviews upstream ducts instead of implying inevitable dilatation, and interprets wall findings with acquisition/clinical context. The 1600 px rendering was directly inspected for label readability and layout. Its conceptual shapes provide no source anatomical or fine wall/duct coverage credit.
