# Peripheral MRA reporting and representation requirements

`ra.mra-peripheral-vessels` now requires **88 groups / 925 leaves / 2,775 image, schematic and model obligations**. These are draft requirements awaiting independent peripheral vascular MRI/clinical review, not approved coverage. They include both actually covered vessel trees, origins and variants, relevant profunda/popliteal branches, tibial/peroneal runoff, dorsal/plantar connections and distal branches, collateral routes, intervention anatomy, disease components and adjacent findings. Every acquired additional peripheral territory still needs its complete named inventory; the lower-limb floor does not substitute for another examination or make the complete total known.

The five reporting fields require actual source-supported patency, lesion/reference measurements, branch and endpoint tracing, and per-station source quality. Four prior normal assertions have been removed. A nonvisualised vessel remains uncertain when coverage, timing, flow, metal, overlap, variant anatomy or subtraction can explain it. Three-vessel runoff does not establish pedal continuity, a complete arch, wound perfusion or tissue viability. Quantitative flow/pressure and clinical effects require their own sources.

The investigation-specific aorta companion explicitly lacks the patient-specific iliac-to-pedal tree, grafts, lesions, arterial wall and measured flow. No geometry is credited from that parent. Historical scanner, injection/compression, treatment and pixel-count statements from the original RA chapter are not adopted as universal current practice or clinical fidelity thresholds. The 2024 multisociety PAD context informs urgent clinical assessment without delaying care for routine MRA; this guide does not make individual intervention or amputation decisions.

Source review is limited to the effective guide and the listed primary excerpts in `peripheral-mra-source-review.json`. Images/pixels/series and reuse rights were not approved in this turn. The pedal anatomy review concerns ultrasound technique, the popliteal paper's abstract establishes variability, and the pedal MRA study supplies research image-quality context; none become a different modality's acquired examination or complete current clinical approval. Failed full-text/PDF retrievals are explicit. The existing three source images and native distal vessel/pathology models still require fidelity and rights work.
