# Original clinical pancreatitis figures in the reader

The local acute-pancreatitis lesson now includes **seven complete original published figures** from Binit Sureka, Kalpana Bansal, Yashwant Patidar and Ankur Arora, *Imaging lexicon for acute pancreatitis: 2012 Atlanta Classification revisited*, DOI [10.1093/gastro/gov036](https://doi.org/10.1093/gastro/gov036), [PMC4760067](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4760067/). These are source-labelled clinical examples, not complete acquired studies or independently approved anatomical boundaries.

The original article XML explicitly grants **CC BY 4.0**, with author/copyright attribution retained in every reader figure. Original captions and figure elements were inspected for excluded third-party credits; none were found for the selected figures. XML and repository media matched their published MD5 values from NLM/PMC Article Dataset metadata. Other candidates with noncommercial or conditional research-only permissions were not substituted for this grant. Rights review does not approve anatomy or make a public article a native DICOM dataset.

Original repository JPEGs remain byte-for-byte unchanged for Figures 1/3/4/6/8. The verified original publication PDF embeds larger complete figure JPEGs for Figures 5 (951 × 497 rather than 800 × 418) and 7 (1001 × 252 rather than 798 × 201); those original DCT streams were extracted without pixel recompression. An independent PDF-object readback compared object 47 and object 54 directly with the packaged JPEGs: both matched byte-for-byte. No clinical figure is cropped, resized, relabelled, sharpened, painted or re-annotated. Larger dimensions do not prove a higher-resolution original acquisition or fine reporting-anatomy accuracy.

Mixed modalities are explicit in captions, source panel types, selected clinical-panel lists and ancillary records. The reader validator now permits MRI/CT as explicit ancillary types as well as its existing types. The fidelity gate rejects an MRI panel claimed as selected CT evidence. Original panel letters, arrows and complete layout remain visible. Source states and authors' diagnostic/sequence descriptions remain source assertions; no independent histology, calibrated timing or patient-specific 3D reconstruction is inferred.
