# Human tumour–muscularis propria tissue evidence

Jansen et al., *Three-dimensional histopathological reconstruction of bladder tumours*, Diagnostic Pathology14,25(2019), [DOI10.1186/s13000-019-0803-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13000-019-0803-7), provides distinct fine-tissue context beyond the existing gross bladder anatomy and MRI tumour-cell annotations. The full original XML grant is CC BY4.0, with no separately restricted credit in selected Figures1,4,6. Original article XML/PDF and selected repository media match the NLM publisher MD5s. The complete source article and three figure/page bindings were reviewed.

The larger original PDF image objects44,62,68 on pages3,4,5 are used at1420×609,1420×752,896×604 pixels. Every decoded RGB value agrees with independent Poppler extraction. The original ICC RGB profile is preserved in lossless PNG. Source artwork, outlines, panel letters, source scale bars and white backgrounds remain, with no crop, resampling, enhancement or relabelling. This preserves published image samples; it does not recover whole-slide resolution or independently calibrate tissue colour or distances. The original publisher object and page references remain in the source review.

Figure1 has two unlettered positions: the left photograph shows the annotation workstation; the right digitized tissue has original tumour outlines in red and muscularis propria in orange. The article does not number that specimen; it is not silently identified as specimen1. The workstation image is ancillary photography, not an additional histological acquisition.

Figure4 is source specimen1, reported as papillary low-grade (grade2) Ta with muscularis propria present. The original a/b panels are flat rendered tissue/detail views, and c is the aligned stack view. The source's1000/250/500 micrometre scale bars are retained as source annotations. Figure6 explicitly refers to specimen1: tumour above, muscularis propria below, and deliberately blurred intervening tissue. These figures may be compared as one source specimen; neither is registered to TCGA-DK-AA6P, FedBCa, HRA or the current learner's report. This is a separated Ta example, not a displayed proof of muscular invasion.

The specimens were formalin fixed and paraffin embedded, cut into4–5 micrometre sections and stained with H&E. Every second section was mounted. Original scanned slide sampling was reported as0.5 micrometres/pixel; that number is not the pixel scale of these publication figures. The authors used rigid, affine and nonrigid B-spline alignment and qualitatively assessed registration. Excluded slides could be replaced with duplicate neighbours. Only portions of tumour blocks were sectioned, and fixation, cutting, mounting and staining can change tissue appearances and geometry. The datasets are expressly not publicly available. The article's data waiver does not create access to absent native arrays or masks.

The bladder source gallery retains Histology/ex-vivo scope, original captions, specimen and processing notes, full-source zoom and attribution. Published renderings are flat evidence, with no inferred mesh, native3D or pathology confirmation. No new structure requirements receive coverage or anatomical/clinical approval. Whole-wall interfaces, fine anatomical extent and all-radiology clinical/commercial readiness remain unproven.
