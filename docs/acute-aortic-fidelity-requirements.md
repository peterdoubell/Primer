# Acute aortic syndrome: full reporting-scope draft

Reviewed 6 October 2026. The complete effective five-field CT acute-aortic reference now has **85 groups, 750 separately reportable leaves and 2,250 image/schematic/model obligations**. All actual lesions, communications, variants, branches, affected territories and repair components require site-specific instantiation. This is a known floor, not independent anatomical approval.

The inventory covers root/sinuses/annulus and each aortic segment; true/false-lumen courses, flap attachments, entry/re-entry/limited tears, intramural haemorrhage and blood pools, ulcerative lesions and thrombus; coronary/arch/carotid/subclavian/vertebral, visceral, paired renal/accessory and iliac/femoral branches; actual intercostal/spinal supply and covered brain/renal/visceral/myocardial/limb territories; rupture and pericardial/mediastinal/pleural/abdominal compartments; adjacent pulmonary/airway/oesophageal/venous interfaces; conditional repair devices and complications; and source coverage, phases, gating, comparison/calibration and functional limits.

Three unsupported normal presets became actual assessment/coverage prompts. Classic Stanford wording now depends on ascending involvement rather than a mandatory distal-left-subclavian start; named arch-only/non-A/non-B frameworks and actual extent remain explicit. Lumen size is not definitive identity, static branch extension does not prove dynamic obstruction or clinically confirmed malperfusion syndrome, and pericardial volume alone does not diagnose tamponade. Immediate suspected-AAS/rupture/malperfusion communication remains unchanged.

Sources include the effective Radiology Assistant reference, the original ECG-gated pictorial source (DOI 10.1007/s13244-012-0195-7), [ACC/AHA 2022 classification extracts](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9860464/) and [STS/AATS 2022 mechanism/syndrome extracts](https://www.sts.org/sites/default/files/content/TBAD_Guideline_2022.pdf). Extract review is not full contemporary guideline or multidisciplinary review. CT morphology alone cannot establish valve function, tamponade physiology, time-varying flow, end-organ viability, treatment suitability or outcome.

Reproduce with: python -m tools.anatomy_sources.expand_acute_aortic_requirements.
