How the picture is calculated

The round, opaque blocker is 12 cm wide. The screen sits 100 cm from the lamp. At the screen point x, project the blocker back onto the lamp with straight lines. If the blocker is at fraction f of the lamp-to-screen distance, the projected blocker has radius R/(1 − f) and is centred at −fx/(1 − f), where R is the blocker radius.

The screen diagram shades each point by the fraction of the round lamp left visible. The model treats light as straight rays and represents source area. It omits diffraction, scattered light and the variation of light intensity with distance and angle.

The fully blocked region is called the umbra. The partially blocked region is the penumbra. The same hiding geometry is visible during a solar eclipse, when the Moon covers some or all of the Sun.
