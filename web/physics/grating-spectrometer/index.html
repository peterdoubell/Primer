Try a controlled comparison. With groove density and order fixed, opening more grooves narrows the lines without moving their principal directions. Increasing density at fixed N spreads the directions apart and broadens the angular peaks too, leaving |m|N unchanged. Larger pixels can erase detail even when the grating meets the ideal criterion.

What is measured, and what is modelled?

NIST tabulates these neutral-sodium lines at 5889.950 Å and 5895.924 Å in air, equivalent to 588.9950 and 589.5924 nm. They are reference wavelengths from the Handbook’s R56 source, P. Risberg (1956). The Handbook points to newer, higher-accuracy sodium references. This activity preserves the stated digits and supplies no invented measurement uncertainty.

The profiles, pixel readings and apparatus are an ideal instrument model, not a captured lamp spectrum or measured detector data. The two mutually incoherent wavelength components have equal normalized weights here; NIST’s tabulated relative intensities are not assumed to be universal lamp powers.

Equations, dimensions and approximations

At normal incidence, principal directions obey d sin θ = mλ, using the wavelength in air and groove spacing in metres. At |mλ/d| = 1 an order is grazing; above 1 it is non-propagating. Neither is shown as a forward telescope image in this model. The selected telescope points at the mean of the two principal angles; its ideal rectilinear coordinate is x = f tan(θ − θ₀). The sensor is 1.2 mm wide. Its assigned wavelength at a pixel centre is d sin[θ₀ + arctan(x/f)] / m.

For N identical, uniformly illuminated, very narrow grooves, the scalar far-field array factor is I(θ) = [sin(Nπq)/(N sin πq)]² with q = d sin θ / λ. Its limiting value at integer q is 1. This is the normalized interference factor: groove efficiency, finite groove-width envelope, entrance-slit width, aberrations, Doppler/pressure/hyperfine structure, detector noise and absolute flux are omitted. It is not a full real-grating efficiency or lens design.

An ideal focusing telescope accesses angular diffraction; this is not a claim that a bare screen a short distance from a large grating is in the Fraunhofer regime. Local detector angles are mapped by the stated ideal x relation. Every pixel is averaged numerically across its width, with at least 24 samples per conservative minimum fringe pitch fλ/(Nd). Profiles are not smoothed or shifted to force a double peak. The near-equal-line, narrow-separation Rayleigh estimate R = |m|N is a grating criterion, separate from detector sampling and other instrument limits.
