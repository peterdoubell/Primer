Why these intervals and point types follow

When a is positive, x² − a factors as (x − √a)(x + √a). Its roots divide the line into positive, negative and positive derivative intervals. At −√a, f changes from increasing to decreasing. At √a, it changes from decreasing to increasing.

At a = 0, f′(x) = 3x² is positive on either side of zero. The function keeps increasing through its horizontal tangent. Its second derivative, f″(x) = 6x, changes sign there.

When a is negative, x² − a is positive for every real x. The graphs show the window −3 ≤ x ≤ 3. The sign table describes the whole polynomial.

Enclose the most area with 40 m of fencing

Use all 40 m for the four sides of a rectangle. If its width is w, its length is 20 − w. Change the width and compare the plan, area and rate of area change.

A(w) = w(20 − w) m². Its derivative is 20 − 2w m² per metre of width, and its second derivative is −2. The closed comparison domain includes w = 0 and w = 20 m, where the rectangle collapses to a line and has zero area.

The identity A(w) = 100 − (w − 10)² proves that every allowed width gives area at most 100 m². Equality occurs at w = 10 m, with length 10 m. The deficit from the maximum is (w − 10)² m².
