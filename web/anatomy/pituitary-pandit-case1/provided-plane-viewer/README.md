# Provided pituitary tumour/carotid MRI source: case 1 preflight

The primary publisher dataset is Pandit et al., *Mapping pituitary neuroendocrine tumors: an annotated MRI dataset profiling tumor and carotid characteristics*, Figshare 27894084 version 1, DOI [10.6084/m9.figshare.27894084.v1](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.27894084.v1). Its own publisher metadata applies [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) to the dataset. The separate source paper is [Scientific Data 12, 80 (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-04218-8). Full author and dataset metadata are retained in `figshare-27894084-v1.json`.

The 47,245,902,155-byte source archive was inspected with bounded, ETag-guarded HTTP ranges. Reading 56,120 bytes yielded the complete 533-entry ZIP index. Case 1 was selected as the first numbered source case with T1, T2 and both annotation files, not as a representative patient or a case selected for favourable agreement. Its four original files were recovered with source ZIP CRC checks, per-file SHA256 and the same provider object ETag. No full-archive checksum is claimed: the publisher's archive MD5 field was empty, and the complete 47 GB archive was not downloaded.

The original provided MRI files are cached only under `.research/pituitary-case-source-20261101/case-1`; their hashes, byte counts, source archive offsets and recovery ranges are in the acquisition receipt. This Git evidence packet retains the two original small annotation files. No data were uploaded to hosting or integrated into the application.

The source images are processed research grids, not acquisition-native MRI. The authors report reorientation, bias correction, T2 registration, isotropic resampling and face cropping. They also describe annotation supersampling, Gaussian smoothing, erosion and removal of stray voxels. The delivered case 1 images and masks are 499×404×342 at 0.5 mm. No additional image resampling, fitted registration, smoothing, erosion, component removal or header correction was applied by Primer.

The original files contain separate binary annotations for the tumour–gland complex and combined intracranial carotids. The tumour/gland mask must not be relabelled as independently separated tumour and normal gland. The carotid file supplies no independent wall, branch or laterality labels. The delivered annotations contain 6,416 and 13,196 positive cells respectively, with one and two 26-neighbour components; no source-mask overlap was found. These are source-sample facts, not biological volume, completeness or diagnostic-accuracy approvals.

Both images have matching coded sform/qform frames. The annotation sforms match the image grid to about 1.57×10^-9mm over grid corners; their coded qforms instead shift 22.5 mm relative to the images. A tiny quaternion/diagonal basis difference is retained as well. `case-1-source-review.json` preserves every matrix and the separate coded alternatives. The comparison figure uses original image samples and nearest original-mask display lookup under each declared form. It does not repair the headers, fit a corrective transform or save a resampled mask. The qform alternative displaces the displayed annotations relative to the supplied MRI; this source correspondence issue remains explicit.

Primer has not granted this case clinical/anatomical approval. Its independently reviewed source status, preprocessing, source-grid limitations, coordinate conflict, label granularity and actual module bindings must be resolved explicitly before a reader is released. It must never be registered or fused with the separate SPL atlas by presentation alone.

The original workbook has repeated voxel/matrix column headings. `case-1-source-metadata.json` retains every selected cell with its original column and heading, without collapsing duplicates. Case 1 is source-reported as Micro, Cushing's and Knosp 0; this context is not inferred from the images. Its source mask volume (802 mm³) agrees with the workbook value but is not an independently established isolated tumour volume. Missing acquisition fields remain missing. The full cohort workbook remains in the ignored source cache; publisher MD5/SHA256 records and original inert processing scripts are retained.

The workbook reports case 1 T1 acquisition at 0.488 × 0.488 × 1.5 mm (512 × 512 × 114), despite the provided isotropic 0.5 mm grid. Resampling does not establish newly acquired through-plane detail. Case-specific T2 acquisition fields are unspecified, even though the processed T2 file is supplied.

The subsequent lossless provided-plane package, portable verification planes, model packages and controlled memory-bounded reader evidence are in `provided-plane-reader/`. All three plane families reconstruct the supplied volumes exactly. This remains a source-data preview with the same unresolved header/tissue-granularity limits and no new clinical approval or application binding.
