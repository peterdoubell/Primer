Inspect complete released TRACE, ADC, T1-weighted and FLAIR grids and all three original lesion masks. Click a voxel or use arrow keys; Page Up/Down moves its section and Shift moves five samples. Every readout preserves the stored integer, header-scaled value and oblique RAS coordinate.

Source extent, registration, interpretation and reuse

All seven complete arrays are retained. TRACE, ADC and masks load first. T1 or FLAIR loads in full when selected; the previous large anatomical buffer is released before loading the next. The availability readout identifies each complete buffer currently loaded. No crop or lower-resolution substitute is used. TRACE, ADC and three source masks have identical stored spatial affines for this case, allowing linked source-index inspection. T1 and FLAIR each retain a separate grid and cursor; no registration transform is supplied between them or to diffusion, and neither accepts a mask overlay. Source T1/FLAIR sampling is finer than diffusion, but scanner reconstruction, source acquisition limits and defacing are preserved.

The primary article describes manual source labels by three trained neuroscientists, but its methods refer inconsistently to T2, ADC and TRACE. Its Data Records and original filenames specify TRACE-space labels. “Acute”, “chronic” and “combined” are source-file descriptions, not a new clinical determination. A binary mask is not a complete representation of injury heterogeneity. Source masks and every source value are unchanged.

Adaptations: reversible lossless transport of the stored source samples; display-only windowing and tint; literal source voxel-cell triangulation in the exact selected affine. Float64 geometry is retained and verified; GPU rendering uses float32 display coordinates. Clinical and fine anatomical review remain pending.
