    A speaker can combine credibility, emotion and reasoning. These sentences concern Harbour Library’s proposal. Inspect them separately from the map’s inferential links.

    A repeatable argument check
    1. Write the actual conclusion and proposal in full, preserving their scope.
    2. Separate what the record establishes from assumptions and claims about it.
    3. State the link that would let the premises support the conclusion.
    4. Check for a changed proposal, omitted alternatives or an unsupported causal claim.
    5. Repair the claim or identify the additional evidence needed, keeping the remaining uncertainty visible.

    The feedback concerns these supplied arguments and records. Words such as “either” or “after” can occur in sound reasoning too. Judge the stated alternatives and support in context.
