How the score works

  1. Choose a split and one passage. The diagram has two interior nodes and five edges.
  2. Try each observed reading at each interior node. Count one change across every edge whose two readings differ. An unreadable leaf can match its neighbour and adds zero.
  3. Keep the lowest count for that passage. Add the counts for the selected passages and compare the three totals.

The score assumes equal change costs and a single tree for all selected passages. Repeated changes and borrowing between branches can produce conflicting readings. The diagram has no root, so its edges give no direction of copying and cannot identify an author's wording.
