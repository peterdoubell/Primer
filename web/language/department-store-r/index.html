How often did speakers pronounce /r/?

Compare the first replies and careful repetitions in William Labov’s 264 department-store interviews in New York City in 1962.

How the replies were elicited

  1. Ask for directions

    The interviewer asks about a department on the fourth floor.

  2. Listen to the first reply

    “Fourth floor.” Both words give a place to listen for /r/.

  3. Request repetition

    “Excuse me?” prompts a more careful reply, sometimes just “Fourth.”
